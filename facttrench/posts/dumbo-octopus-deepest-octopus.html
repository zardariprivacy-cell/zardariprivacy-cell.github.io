The Dumbo Octopus: The Cutest Animal in the Ocean's Darkest Place

2026-11-21 · 3 min read · By FactTrench

It looks like a cartoon that escaped into the real ocean. The Dumbo octopus (Grimpoteuthis) swims by flapping two fins on the sides of its head that look exactly like the ears of Disney's famous elephant — and it lives in one of the most hostile places on the planet.

The deepest octopus on Earth

Dumbo octopuses are the deepest-living octopuses known. They are usually found between 3,000 and 4,800 meters down, and in 2020 a Dumbo-type octopus was filmed at 6,957 meters in the Java Trench — deeper than the wreck of the Titanic, in total darkness and near-freezing water.

No ink. No problem.

Ordinary octopuses squirt ink to escape predators. The Dumbo octopus has no ink sac at all — in permanent darkness, a cloud of ink fools nobody. Instead, it simply flies away on its ear-fins, or folds its webbed arms into an umbrella shape and pulses through the black water.

It also eats differently: with almost no beak strength for tearing, it pounces on snails, worms and small crustaceans and swallows them whole. Down in the abyss, meals are rare — you do not chew what might be your only food for weeks.

There are at least 17 known species of Dumbo octopus, and scientists keep finding more. The cutest face in the ocean, it turns out, belongs to one of its toughest neighborhoods.

Quick Facts

  • Named for its ear-like fins, which it flaps to swim
  • Deepest octopus group: commonly 3,000–4,800 m; filmed at 6,957 m in 2020
  • It has no ink sac — ink is useless in total darkness
  • It swallows its prey whole, in a single gulp
  • At least 17 known species of Grimpoteuthis

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is it called the Dumbo octopus?

Because of the two fins on its head that look like the big ears of Dumbo, Disney's cartoon elephant. It flaps them to fly through the water.

How deep does the Dumbo octopus live?

Most live 3,000–4,800 meters deep. In 2020, one was filmed at 6,957 meters in the Java Trench — the deepest octopus sighting ever recorded.

Does the Dumbo octopus have ink?

No. Unlike shallow-water octopuses, it has no ink sac. In the pitch-black deep sea, ink provides no protection, so evolution removed it.
