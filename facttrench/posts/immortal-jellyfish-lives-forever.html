This Jellyfish Can Live Forever — By Turning Back Into a Baby

2026-11-21 · 3 min read · By FactTrench

Every animal you know follows the same story: born, grow up, grow old, die. Every animal — except one. The immortal jellyfish, Turritopsis dohrnii, a creature smaller than your fingernail, can press a reset button on its own life.

The reset button

When this jellyfish becomes old, sick, or injured, it sinks to the ocean floor and collapses into a blob. From that blob, it transforms back into its baby stage — a polyp, the very first stage of a jellyfish's life — and then grows up all over again. Its cells literally change jobs in the process, a trick scientists call transdifferentiation.

In theory, it can repeat this cycle forever. That makes Turritopsis dohrnii the only known animal that is biologically immortal — it never has to die of old age.

Immortal, not invincible

Forever is a long time, and the ocean is hungry. Immortal jellyfish still get eaten by sea slugs, fish and turtles, and they still die from disease. Biological immortality only means one thing: old age itself cannot kill them.

First described in the Mediterranean Sea, this tiny jellyfish has quietly spread to oceans all over the world, hitching rides in the ballast water of ships — an immortal stowaway, colonizing the planet one reset at a time. Scientists now study its cells for clues about aging itself.

Quick Facts

  • Scientific name: Turritopsis dohrnii — only about 4–5 mm wide
  • When stressed or old, it reverts to its baby (polyp) stage and regrows
  • The cell trick behind it is called transdifferentiation
  • It is the only known biologically immortal animal
  • It can still be eaten or die of disease — only aging cannot kill it

Frequently Asked Questions

Can the immortal jellyfish really live forever?

In theory, yes — it can restart its life cycle indefinitely, so it never dies of old age. In practice, most are eaten or get sick long before forever arrives.

How does the immortal jellyfish restart its life?

It sinks to the floor, collapses into a cyst-like blob, and transforms back into a polyp — its baby stage — through transdifferentiation, where adult cells convert into different cell types.

Where does the immortal jellyfish live?

It was first found in the Mediterranean Sea and has since spread to temperate and tropical oceans worldwide, largely by traveling in ships' ballast water.
